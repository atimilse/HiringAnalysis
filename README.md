# Hiring Analysis: Identifying the Factors Behind Hiring Decisions

**A data analytics project that uses descriptive statistics and data visualization to understand which candidate characteristics are associated with being hired at a global technology company, and how an organization can use those insights to improve recruitment.**

## Overview

Hiring is costly and time-consuming, and the challenge grows as demand for skilled technology talent rises. Longer hiring cycles increase operational costs and leave critical roles unfilled, while decisions that are not supported by data risk poor fits and longer onboarding. This project analyzes candidate data to identify which characteristics are associated with hiring outcomes and turns the findings into practical recommendations for recruiting teams.

## Questions Explored

- Do hired candidates have more LinkedIn connections than candidates who were not hired?
- Are candidates without a LinkedIn profile less likely to be hired?
- Does age affect hiring decisions?
- Do hired candidates have more years of experience?
- Do higher machine learning self-scores increase hiring chances?
- Do hired candidates score higher on technical assessments?

## Approach

1. **Data integration:** Combined four candidate datasets covering basic information, online presence, technical skills, and interview evaluations into a single analysis-ready dataset, using standardized column names and a shared candidate ID.
2. **Descriptive analysis:** Compared hired and rejected candidates using averages, medians, quartiles, and standard deviations.
3. **Visualization:** Used histograms, violin plots, and bar charts to show how the two groups differ.
4. **Recommendations:** Translated findings into clear, actionable guidance for recruiting and hiring teams.

## Key Takeaways

- **Online professional presence matters.** Hired candidates tended to have a LinkedIn profile and a higher number of connections.
- **Experience stands out.** Hired candidates were generally older and had substantially more years of experience than rejected candidates.
- **Technical skills are rewarded.** Hired candidates tended to score higher on both the technical assessment and machine learning self-scores.
- **Together, these factors point to a clear profile:** experienced, technically strong candidates with an established professional presence are hired at higher rates.

## Recommendations

1. Prioritize candidates' professional online presence during screening.
2. Define clear, role-specific minimum experience requirements before the interview stage.
3. Set performance benchmarks and use customized technical testing, supported by role-specific assessments rather than self-ratings alone.

## Limitations and Next Steps

- Self-reported skill scores can be biased or inconsistent between candidates.
- Interviewer scoring may vary without a standardized rubric.
- The analysis focused on exploring relationships. A natural next step is building predictive models to forecast hiring decisions, supported by standardized data collection.
